Why I'd be a strong scout for Inovia

Inovia wants someone who takes initiative, connects people, and can tell when a team is onto something. Here is where that already shows up in my work.

  • I'm already sourcing startups for venture funds.

    Forum Ventures, with Sony Innovation Fund and Comma Capital coming up

    Through RCVC, I screen pre-seed B2B SaaS and agentic AI startups for Forum Ventures. I look at whether customers really need the product, how fast revenue could grow, and how founders handle setbacks. I'm also about to start sourcing projects for Sony Innovation Fund and Comma Capital. Scouting for Inovia uses the same habits, for a firm that can back a company from pre-seed all the way to IPO.

  • I care more about other people's ideas than my own.

    SC Ventures, RCVC research team

    At SC Ventures' Young Entrepreneur Program in Singapore, my team pitched a VR headset and won the Creative Genius Award. But the part I enjoyed most was listening to the other teams and asking myself which one I would back. That is what pulled me into venture capital. Today I co-lead RCVC's venture research team, where student analysts bring their work to me and we turn it into research for partner funds.

  • I can keep up with technical founders.

    ArcTern Ventures, Deloitte Taiwan

    I study finance and economics with a data science focus and a CS minor. With ArcTern Ventures, I researched AI chips and argued that time to first token is a better way to judge inference hardware than energy efficiency alone. At Deloitte, I designed IoT and physical AI setups for Taiwan's national port operator. So when a Vector researcher or a UofT engineer explains what they're building, I can follow along and ask good questions. That matters for a firm that backs AI companies like Cohere.

  • My network reaches past Toronto.

    Hsinchu, Taipei, Singapore

    I grew up in Hsinchu, the home of Taiwan's chip industry, and I speak English and Mandarin. This summer at Deloitte in Taipei, I worked with clients across Taiwan and dug through company reports from Japan, Korea, Thailand, and Vietnam. Before that I was an investment analyst intern at Skymont Capital in Singapore. When a Toronto team is building hardware or selling into Asia, I can help Inovia understand that side of the story.

How I'd spend the semester

October to December 2026, with the option to keep going from January to April 2027 if it's working for both of us.

  1. October

    Get set up and start sending

    Meet the Toronto team and agree on the sectors and stages you care about most. Send my first companies in week one, then keep a steady pace of one to three a week.

  2. November

    Host the event

    Plan and run a founder dinner at Inovia's Toronto office with someone from the Inovia team, while the weekly notes keep coming.

  3. December

    Look back honestly

    Share what I sent, what reached a 30-minute screen, and what I got wrong. Then we decide together whether to extend.

What each weekly note will include

Short enough to read in two minutes, with enough detail to decide on a screen.

The company
Name, and one plain sentence on what they build
The signal
Users, revenue, key hires, or a raise I can point to
The founders
Who they are and why I think they're strong
Why Inovia
Stage, sector, and why now is the right time
How I know
Met them myself, or heard from someone close to the team

My event idea: an AI builders dinner

About 20 people around one table. Student founders from UofT, researchers at Vector who are thinking about starting a company, and one or two Inovia portfolio founders who can talk about raising their first round. Small enough that everyone meets everyone, which is where the best introductions come from.

How I judge a team

Three questions first. Do customers actually need this? Can revenue grow quickly? Will these founders keep going when things get hard? I'd rather send one company I can defend than three I can't.
